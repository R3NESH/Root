## 1. Wall joins — `feat/wall-combine-joins`

Drawn walls combine into one run with square, rounded or chamfered corners. The run is stored on
its own walls as a `chainId`, so it saves and undoes with them rather than needing its own store;
`resolveChainWalls` turns walls-as-drawn into walls-as-built and feeds both the 3D scene and the
2D blueprint from one resolution. `frontend/lib/wallJoins.ts`, 510 lines.

Four follow-up fixes the same day, each worth recording because each is a UI failure that
`tsc` cannot see:

- A picked wall and its whole run now draw blue. Before, there was no way to tell what you had.
- Shift means "select" everywhere. It did not, so a run could not be picked at all.
- A solved-plan wall says **why** it cannot join a run instead of silently declining.
- The 3D run panel was rendering underneath the tool HUD.

## 4. The room maximums were test fixtures too

[[room-sizes-from-code]] closed this for the *minimums* on 2026-09-03 and did not look at the
other end. `bench_realism` says a 2BHK filled **46% of a 40×60** and a 4BHK **33% of a 50×80**,
both binding on the catalog rather than on the plot — **a bigger plot drew the same small house
in a bigger footprint.** That is the whole of the long-running "why does the interior look
cramped" complaint, and it was measurable the entire time.

`WALL_HEIGHT_FT` 9.0 → 10.0 at the same time: nine feet is the NBC *minimum*, and every interior
had been drawn at the legal floor.

## Findings

- **A documented invariant that the code does not enforce is not an invariant.**
  `api/main._size_range()` carried a comment saying neither source widens the catalog, and
  returned the pin unclamped **one line below**. Seventeen shipped blueprints were under a floor
  the solver enforces as a hard constraint — a 7×6 bedroom at half the NBC habitable area, three
  kitchens 6 ft wide. The comment had been right and wrong in the same function for weeks.
- **A stair is the one catalog object that must never be scaled.** `PlacedCustomObject.scale`
  multiplies riser and going together; scale a compliant stair up and the riser passes 190 mm,
  scale it down and the going drops under 250 mm. What should change with the space available is
  the *number* of steps, not their size. That is why `stairPath.ts` exists at all.
- **The catalog was test fixtures at both ends**, and only one end was checked in September.
  Worth asking of every other range in the code: what was it tuned against?

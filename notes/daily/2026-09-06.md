# Daily Note — 2026-09-06

Session started from one question — "what is the most important thing we're missing" — and ended
with two locked decisions reversed on the user's call. Three features shipped, one built and
thrown away, one pre-existing test flake measured and left alone.

## 1. Requested room pairs — a preference, scored

A `near` array of index pairs on `POST /solve`. Pairs the person asked to keep together are minimised as
centre-to-centre Manhattan distance in the CP-SAT objective, not posted as a constraint. The
reasoning and why it does not soften rules-as-constraints are in [[preferences-are-scored]].

**`NEAR_WEIGHT = 60`, measured.** Six-room mix, 30x40 north-facing, one kitchen↔bedroom
pair, three cold runs each. Weight 0 is the same solve with no pair requested:

| weight | pair centres apart | fill/ceiling | void | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 197 in | 84% | 4% | FEASIBLE |
| 30 | 172 in | 84% | 4% | FEASIBLE |
| **60** | **153 in** | **81%** | **4%** | FEASIBLE |
| 120 | 121 in | 77% | 9% | FEASIBLE/OPTIMAL |
| 250 | 102 in | 72% | 18% | FEASIBLE |
| 500 | 102 in | 72% | 18% | FEASIBLE |

The cost is paid in room area and in void inside the footprint, and the two do not arrive
together. Up to 60 the void does not move at all — the pair is satisfied by choosing among
arrangements the compactness term already liked. At 120 the void more than doubles: past that
the solver is pulling rooms together by making the building straggle. 250 saturates.

Threaded end to end — the frontend holds pairs as room **ids**, not indices, because the app
rebuilds its mix in `ROOM_NAMES` order and the model's indices would point at the wrong rooms
by the time the solver saw them.

## 2. Size drift — built, measured, deleted

Held each room's width and depth across an edit alongside its position, at `DRIFT_WEIGHT`. Then
ran the check [[claim-most-likely-wrong]] insists on: does the problem exist? Ten perturbed edits
of the standard five-room mix, total room size change:

```
         prev only: 1 in
  prev + prev_size: 0 in
```

**One inch.** Position drift plus non-overlap already pins size in practice. The term cost an API
field, a solver parameter and an objective loop for nothing, so it went. Where sizes *do* change
across an edit — widening the kitchen makes a bedroom give up a foot — the change is forced by
geometry and the term did not prevent that either.

## 3. Two locked decisions reversed — [[free-text-input]]

Both on the user's explicit call after the collision was put to them:

- **Chatbot / conversational input**, rejected 2026-08-23 under [[zero-keyboard-events]].
- **Parking porch, sit-out, utility as room kinds**, removed 2026-08-25. The staircase stays
  rejected as a kind; `open_sided` came back with the porch and the sit-out.

Struck through rather than deleted in [[rejected-approaches]]: a decision that was reversed is
not a decision that was never made.

### The catalog

`sitout`, `parking`, `utility` back in `solver/rooms.py`, sourced rather than guessed — NBC 2016
puts a common car space at 2.5 x 5.0 m and an individual one at 3.0 x 6.0 m. The porch is pinned
to the street edge: a porch no car can reach is not a porch.

`open_sided` is respected in four places, and the fourth is the one that matters — the daylight
rule still drives them to the outside face, `walls.py` emits no exterior wall, `take_off()`
therefore bills no bricks, and `Scene.tsx` builds no wall mesh where the solver emitted none.
Without that last one a car porch renders as a garage and the quantities bill for masonry nobody
laid.

Balcony deliberately not added: it is a sit-out on an upper floor and needs floor-aware catalog
rules that do not exist.

### The free-text layer

`backend/ai/` → `POST /ai/plan`, which returns a `/solve` body, **not a plan**. The model does
vocabulary; CP-SAT still places every room, so zoning stays a constraint a language model cannot
overrule. Two rules it will not break: nothing is silently dropped (`unsupported` comes back and
the UI shows it), and there is no offline path (503 without a credential — a regex parser
standing in would be [[client-side-fallback]] wearing a hat).

### The failing test is not a regression, and here is the measurement

It asserts fill ≥ 90% of the catalog ceiling. Four runs each, same fixture, standalone:

```
pre-change solver:  93.7%  91.3%  82.7%  86.9%   ← 2 of 4 below the floor
with this session:  88.6%  93.6%  92.8%  93.1%   ← 1 of 4 below
```

It was already marginal on committed code. Under full-suite CPU load it reached 59.5%. It is
measuring the 2 s cold budget, which is exactly what its own comment warns about. Left alone —
loosening the assertion would hide it. It wants a fixed longer budget or deletion;
`test_the_house_reads_as_one_building` already locks down the layout the compactness term buys.

### The headline result — [[claim-most-likely-wrong]] is settled, and the claim HELD

`solver/bench_stability.py`, 5 rooms, 10 perturbed edits:

| Config | Total displacement | Worst jump | Max solve |
|---|---|---|---|
| no drift objective | 2381 in | **1153 in (96 ft)** | 59 ms |
| with drift | **0 in** | 0 in | 66 ms |
| drift + Vaastu | **0 in** | 0 in | 76 ms |

A 96-foot jump is a room teleporting clean across the house. The null hypothesis is dead; the
differentiator survives. It is **not** yet proven a moat — that needs [[q-competitor-defects]].

- **`add_abs_equality` verified.** The one method name in the entire brief nobody had checked.
  It exists and works exactly as [[layout-stability]] wrote it.
- **The predicted blow-up happened**: 962 ms (drift), 1490 ms (drift + Vaastu) vs a 500 ms
  budget. Cause was CP-SAT *proving* optimality, not finding a layout. Fixed by capping the
  interactive solve — the ordering-based fallback was not needed.
- **A latency fix silently broke correctness, and [[test-baseline]] caught it.** Capping *every*
  solve at 0.4 s made hard 6-room packings return `UNKNOWN` — no valid layout at all. Hence two
  budgets: 5 s cold (find *a* layout), 0.4 s interactive (good-enough now). This is precisely
  what the baseline exists for.
- **Camera bug caught in review**: the reframe lived in the geometry rebuild, so every arriving
  solve (~every 400 ms while editing) would have yanked the view from under anyone mid-orbit.
  Moved to its own effect keyed on plot size alone.
- **Containment checked numerically, not by eye.** The screenshot *looks* like rooms overflow
  the dashed envelope; at facing E with 6 rooms it is 0 violations, 0 overlaps. The overhang is
  perspective from the 10 ft extrusion.
- **Known duplication**: setback maths now lives in both `frontend/lib/plot.ts` and
  `backend/envelope/envelope.py`. Verified numerically identical, but nothing enforces that.
